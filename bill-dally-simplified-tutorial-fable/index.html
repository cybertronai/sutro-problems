The Simplified Dally Model

Arithmetic is essentially free. Moving data is what costs energy. Bill Dally (On the Model of Computation, CACM 2022) proposed pricing algorithms by their data movement on a Manhattan grid — here is the simplest possible version of that idea, one add at a time.

The setup

A single processor sits at the origin. Memory is a 2D grid of cells in the upper half-plane around it, packed ring by ring and numbered 1, 2, 3, … spiraling outward. Cell i sits at Manhattan distance ⌈√i⌉ from the processor.

Priced

Reads. Fetching a value from cell i costs its Manhattan distance from the processor.

Priced

Writes. Storing a value back to cell i costs the same Manhattan distance.

Free

Arithmetic. Once operands are in registers, computing on them costs nothing.

When we draw a transfer, the wire always routes horizontally first, then vertically — the length of that L-shaped path is the Manhattan distance, so the picture and the price always agree.

Worked example: 1 + 7

The bill

Why it matters: the add itself was free, yet the computation cost 7 units — every unit was data movement. Scale this intuition up and it explains why real hardware spends most of its energy on wires, caches and DRAM rather than on ALUs, and why good algorithms are the ones that keep their operands close.
