Naive 4×4 Matmul

The baseline triple loop, executed one memory access per clock under the simplified Dally model: an access to address a costs ⌈√a⌉; writes and arithmetic are free.

112 instructions 64 mul · 48 add 240 cycles cost 1,316 best known 800

Layout

The cell at linear index a sits at Manhattan distance ⌈√a⌉ from the core, so ring k holds 2k−1 cells and the whole program fits in seven rings.

A row-major1 – 16d = 1 – 4
B row-major17 – 32d = 5 – 6
C row-major33 – 48d = 6 – 7
tmp49d = 7

Locality across algorithms

Each program is generated and simulated in-page, then plotted per access. Reuse distance is the number of distinct addresses touched since that address was last used — the LRU stack depth, and the thing a scratchpad has to hold to avoid a re-fetch. Access cost is the priced quantity, ⌈√a⌉.

Where the energy goes

Two thirds of the naive bill is scratch traffic. Every one of the 48 accumulate steps reads C[i,j] at distance 6–7 and tmp at distance 7, so tmp alone (336) costs more than every access to A (200). The 800-cost record restructures the loop as outer products, keeping the accumulator and the broadcast operand in the cheapest addresses.
