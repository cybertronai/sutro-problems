01 · Bottom line

It is an excellent binary dot-product engine—not a tiny INT8 multiplier

The instruction wins when the application naturally has one-bit values, reuses or pre-packs its weights, and gives each output enough reduction work. It loses its magic when it is asked to emulate wider arithmetic, repeatedly packs one-shot operands, or writes far more 32-bit output than it reads packed input.

Practical rule

Use B1 for native binary networks, Hamming similarity, sign correlation, binary attention, and other AND/XOR-plus-popcount algorithms. Store static weights in their packed layout and fuse the threshold or next-layer bit pack when possible.

Keep INT8 when values need more than two states. A generic p-bit by q-bit product needs roughly pq binary bit-plane products; the ideal 8× hardware advantage becomes only 8/(pq) before packing and reconstruction overhead.

02 · Exact operation

Match the mathematics before comparing the clocks

The experiment feeds the same pseudorandom 0/1 matrices to both paths. For this domain, signed INT8 multiplication and B1 AND+population-count produce exactly the same S32 dot products; sampled outputs were checked at every shape.

0 / 1 dot product used here Σₖ aₖbₖ = popc(A AND B)

One native binary tensor instruction accumulates a 16×8×256 fragment: 32,768 pair contributions per warp instruction.

−1 / +1 sign dot product a·b = K − 2·popc(A XOR B)

This common BNN interpretation needs XOR plus a scale-and-offset epilogue; it is related, but not the exact kernel measured below.

Native fast path

The compiled SASS contains BMMA.168256.AND.POPC. This is the larger Ampere fragment exposed by inline PTX and CUTLASS, not the older high-level WMMA m8n8k128 path.

Eightfold compute ceiling

NVIDIA’s dense peaks are 624 TOPS for INT8 and 4,992 TOPS for B1 under the same 2MNK operation convention—an exact 8× ratio before memory and launch costs.

Layout is part of the contract

A is row-major packed, B is column-major packed, and the CUTLASS output is column-major S32. A consumer requiring another layout must pay for or fuse that conversion.

The prepacked kernel sustains 2.965 POPS/s, 59.4% of the 4.992 POPS/s dense peak. INT8 sustains 70.5% of its 624 TOPS/s peak.

Output dominates bytes

Minimum traffic is 384 MiB for INT8 versus 272 MiB for B1. The unchanged 256 MiB S32 output is 94% of the B1 byte floor.

Grid coincidence

The existing scorer gives 0.118953 J at 1 fJ per score unit—only 3.1% below measured B1 board energy. This numerical agreement is useful, but not a physical validation of the widthless grid model.

05 · System-level effects

Pack once, reuse often, and do not spill 32-bit intermediates

A fast tensor instruction is only one piece of a useful binary system. The cache experiment uses M=64 and N=K=8192, where one INT8 weight matrix is 64 MiB and one packed B1 matrix is 8 MiB against A100’s 40 MiB L2.

One static weight 6.19× time

10.00× less energy. The 8 MiB packed weight fits in L2; the 64 MiB INT8 weight does not. Measured calls were about 9.1 µs / 0.00106 J for B1 and 57.2 µs / 0.01063 J for INT8.

Eight rotating weights 5.20× time

5.45× less energy. The packed bank is now 64 MiB and the INT8 bank 512 MiB, so neither full bank fits L2. B1 remains much faster, but loses the exceptional hot-weight energy advantage.

Fuse the output boundary

For 8192×8192×256, prepacked B1 is only 1.25× faster and 1.41× lower energy. A fused threshold plus packed next-layer output can remove the 32-bit writeback that causes this ceiling.

Choose the large fragment

The first exploratory kernel used the slower m8n8k128 interface and made B1 look roughly INT8-speed. The final path combines tuned tiling with m16n8k256, which the closest A100 microbenchmark finds at roughly twice the B1 throughput of the smaller fragment.

Quantization has a model cost

Kernel speed does not guarantee accuracy or end-to-end speed. Binary representations often need quantization-aware training, scaling, masks, or selected higher-precision first/last layers.

07 · Physical interpretation

One 1 fJ INT8 grid step corresponds to about 1.25 µm of on-chip travel

The later source is Bill Dally’s Energy Efficiency and AI Hardware keynote at Stanford’s AHA Retreat on August 31, 2023—not a later-2022 summer school. Its rough constants are approximately 100 fJ per bit per millimetre for on-chip communication and 1 fJ per bit for an add, summarized by the slide line “An add is worth 10um of movement.”

The current scorer uses a whole-value unit

One score unit is one source operand times one abstract address-grid step. If we declare that operand to be INT8 and map one score unit to 1 fJ, the distance that costs 1 fJ is:

1 fJ ÷ (8 bits × 100 fJ/bit/mm) = 0.00125 mm = 1.25 µm.

If “1 fJ” were instead charged per bit per step, an INT8 step would cost 8 fJ and correspond to 10 µm. That is not the current scorer convention.

E_move = 100 · b · d_mm fJ d_step = 10 · ε_fJ / b µm b = 8, ε = 1 ⇒ d_step = 1.25 µm

How “big” the grid computer is

The 8192³ schedule occupies 201,359,490 abstract cells, ending at distance ring 14,191. At 1.25 µm per step, the farthest cell is 17.739 mm from compute, the upper-half-diamond base is 35.48 mm, and its idealized area is 38.1% of GA100’s 826 mm².

“7 nm” is not a ruler

GA100 is TSMC N7, but the node name is not a physical wire pitch. TSMC’s N7 platform paper reports roughly 40 nm minimum metal pitch: one 1.25 µm energy-equivalent step spans 31.25 such pitches (or 179 merely nominal “7 nm” labels).

This is an energy equivalence

It is not an SRAM-cell pitch or an A100 wire extraction. Dally’s small-RAM estimate alone is about 50 fJ/bit—400 fJ for INT8—before routers, drivers, clocks, leakage, arithmetic, HBM, and cooling.

Dally’s 2022 CACM antecedent uses 20 fJ for a 32-bit add and 1.9 pJ to move both 32-bit inputs 1 mm, giving 10.53 µm when both move. The later 2023 slide’s rounded 1 fJ/bit and 100 fJ/(bit·mm) constants are not exactly consistent with that two-input calculation. Treat 10 µm as an order-of-magnitude crossover, not a foundry-calibrated law; neither source assigns the rule to a process node.

08 · Measurement boundary

What was—and was not—measured

Hardware

Modal supplied an NVIDIA A100-SXM4-80GB, compute capability 8.0, 400 W limit, driver 580.95.05. The request named A100-40GB; timing and energy identify the actual 80GB device.

Software

PyTorch 2.5.1 + CUDA 12.4 supplies torch._int_mm. B1 uses NVIDIA CUTLASS 3.8.0 at pinned commit afa1772…, fourteen candidate threadblock shapes, and a per-shape best choice.

Energy

The authoritative counter is NVML cumulative board energy. Each stage subtracts the mean of loaded-idle measurements immediately before and after. Results include GPU and HBM, but exclude host and facility energy.

The initial m8-path experiment is not mixed into the tables because it used a slower instruction path, different random INT8 value range, and an A100-40GB. It remains useful as a warning: choosing the wrong fragment can hide essentially all of B1’s compute advantage. All final matched tables use identical {0,1} data and the native m16 path.
